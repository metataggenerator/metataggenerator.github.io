Title Tags: The Complete Guide to Writing Better Titles

The title tag is the single most visible piece of on-page SEO. It becomes the blue clickable headline in Google search results, the text on the browser tab, and the default title when someone bookmarks the page. It is also a ranking factor, which makes it the highest-value line of HTML on most pages.

This guide explains how title tags work, how long they should be, how to write them for both search engines and humans, and the mistakes that waste their power.

How title tags work in search

When Google shows your page in results, the headline usually comes from your title tag. Google may rewrite it if it thinks the tag is misleading, too long, or a poor match for the query, but a good title tag is used as written most of the time. The words in the title also help Google understand what the page is about and which queries it should rank for.

That dual role is what makes title tags special: they serve ranking and clicks at the same time. A title stuffed with keywords may rank, but nobody clicks it. A clever title with no keywords may earn clicks from the few people who see it, but it will not rank for the queries that matter.

How long should a title tag be?

Google shows about 50 to 60 characters of a title tag before cutting it off with an ellipsis. The exact cut depends on pixel width: capital letters and wide characters take more space than narrow ones. Aim for 50 to 60 characters, and put the most important words first, so the cut never removes the key information.

Writing titles that earn clicks

A good title promises a specific benefit or answer. 'How to Fix a Leaking Tap in 10 Minutes' beats 'Plumbing Tips' because it tells the searcher exactly what they will get. Use numbers, years, and specifics where they are honest: they make the result stand out in a list of generic titles.

Match the search intent behind the query. A person searching 'best budget laptop' wants a comparison, so a title that says 'Best Budget Laptops 2026: Tested and Ranked' matches their intent. A person searching 'how to reset a router' wants instructions, so a title starting with 'How to' fits. Write for the intent, not just the keyword.

Common title tag mistakes

The most common mistake is duplicate titles: every page on a site using the same title, often the site name. Each page needs its own title describing its own content. The second mistake is keyword stuffing: repeating the same phrase three times looks spammy and Google may rewrite it.

The third mistake is writing titles for search engines instead of people. A title like 'Cheap Shoes Buy Shoes Online Best Shoes' may contain keywords, but it reads badly and earns few clicks. Write a title a human would want to click, with the keyword included naturally near the start.

Key takeaways

  • Keep title tags to 50-60 characters, with the most important words first.
  • The title tag is a ranking factor and the search headline: write for both.
  • Match the searcher's intent: comparisons, instructions, or answers.
  • Every page needs its own unique title; never repeat one across the site.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a title tag be?

About 50 to 60 characters. Google cuts longer titles off with an ellipsis, so put the most important words first.

Does Google ever change my title tag?

Yes. Google may rewrite titles it considers misleading, too long, or a poor match for the query. A clear, accurate title is usually kept as written.

Should the brand name go in the title tag?

Usually at the end, after a separator such as a pipe or dash. The page's topic should come first, because that is what searchers are looking for.
