Meta Tags for Social Sharing: Make Every Share Look Good

Every share is a small advertisement for your site, shown to people who have never visited it. The preview card decides whether they click or scroll past. The difference between a professional-looking share and a broken one is a handful of meta tags that take minutes to set up.

This guide explains how to make every share look good on Facebook, LinkedIn, X, and messaging apps, and how to keep previews working as your site changes.

The anatomy of a good share

A good share has three parts: a clear image, a title that works on its own, and a one-sentence description that gives a reason to click. The image earns attention, the title earns interest, and the description earns the click. If any part is missing or wrong, the share underperforms.

The tags behind those parts are og:title, og:description, og:image, and og:url, plus the Twitter Card equivalents for X. Set them on every page, and test them before launch, so the first share is already correct.

Platform differences to know

Facebook and LinkedIn use Open Graph tags and show a wide image card. X uses Twitter Card tags, falling back to Open Graph when Twitter tags are missing. WhatsApp and Telegram also use Open Graph, and they show a compact preview in the chat. Each platform crops images slightly differently, so keep important text away from the edges.

LinkedIn is more formal: titles and descriptions that work on Facebook may feel too casual there, so consider a slightly more professional tone for business pages. X is faster-moving: a large image and a short, punchy title work best.

Keeping previews working over time

Previews break when pages change: an image is deleted, a URL changes, or a template update drops the tags. The most common break is a renamed image file that leaves the og:image pointing at a 404. Check your most-shared pages after any redesign, and set up redirects when URLs change.

Platforms cache previews, so a fix may not show immediately. Use the Sharing Debugger for Facebook and the Card Validator for X to refresh the cache after a fix. Test the share yourself on each platform before announcing a new page.

Encouraging people to share

Good tags make shares look professional, but they do not make people share. Add share buttons in visible places: at the top and bottom of articles, and beside products. Write headlines that are worth sharing, and ask readers to share when the content genuinely helps them.

Track which pages get shared most, and study what they have in common. The pattern usually reveals a topic or format your audience loves, and you can make more of it.

Key takeaways

  • Every share needs a clear image, a standalone title, and a one-sentence reason to click.
  • Set Open Graph and Twitter Card tags on every page, not just the homepage.
  • Keep important text away from image edges: platforms crop differently.
  • Refresh cached previews with the Debugger and Validator after any fix.

Frequently asked questions

Which tags control how a shared link looks?

Open Graph tags for Facebook, LinkedIn, and messaging apps, and Twitter Card tags for X. Together they set the image, title, and description.

Why does my shared link look different on each platform?

Each platform crops images and displays cards differently, and each has its own cache. Test on every platform that matters to you.

How do I fix a broken share preview?

Fix the tags or the image, then refresh the cache with Facebook's Sharing Debugger or X's Card Validator.
