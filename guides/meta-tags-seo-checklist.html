Meta Tags SEO Checklist: 15 Things to Check on Every Page

A page can have great content and still underperform because its meta tags are missing, duplicated, or wrong. A checklist turns that risk into a routine: run through it before you publish, and the common errors never reach your live site.

This checklist covers the fifteen checks that matter most, in the order you should run them. It takes about five minutes per page once you know the steps.

Checks 1 to 5: title and description

One, the page has a title tag. Two, the title is 50 to 60 characters and unique to this page. Three, the title includes the page's main keyword near the start and reads naturally. Four, the page has a meta description. Five, the description is 120 to 160 characters, unique, and written as a pitch for the page, not a copy of the first paragraph.

If any of these fail, fix them before publishing. Duplicate titles and descriptions are the most common meta tag errors on real websites, and they are also the easiest to fix.

Checks 6 to 10: social and sharing tags

Six, the page has og:title, og:description, og:image, og:url, and og:type. Seven, the og:image is 1200 by 630 pixels with an absolute URL. Eight, the page has Twitter Card tags, or at least a twitter:card value. Nine, the social title and description work on their own, without a search query for context. Ten, you have tested the preview in the Sharing Debugger and the Card Validator.

Social tags are easy to forget on new page types, such as a new landing page template. Add them to your publishing checklist so they are checked every time, not just when someone remembers.

Checks 11 to 15: technical tags

Eleven, the page has a self-referencing canonical tag with an absolute URL. Twelve, the robots meta tag is absent or set to index, follow, unless the page should be hidden. Thirteen, the charset tag is present and first in the head. Fourteen, the viewport tag is present with width=device-width and initial-scale=1. Fifteen, the page has no meta keywords tag stuffed with words, and no leftover tags from a staging site.

Run the technical checks after any redesign, migration, or template change, because those are the moments when tags get lost or copied from the wrong place.

Making the checklist a habit

Turn the checklist into a template in your publishing workflow: a document, a task list, or a step in your content management system. Assign one person to run it, and have a second person spot-check a sample of pages each month. A checklist nobody owns is a checklist nobody follows.

For large sites, use a crawler to check the whole site at once. Free and paid crawlers report missing, duplicate, and over-long titles and descriptions across thousands of pages, which turns a five-minute-per-page job into a single report.

Key takeaways

  • Check title and description first: present, unique, and the right length.
  • Check social tags and test the preview before publishing.
  • Check canonical, robots, charset, and viewport on every page type.
  • Run the full checklist after any redesign or migration.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a meta tag check take?

About five minutes per page by hand, once you know the steps. A crawler can check a whole site at once.

What is the most common meta tag error?

Duplicate titles and descriptions copied across many pages. Each page needs its own.

Should I check meta tags on every page?

Yes, every important page. Use a crawler for large sites so nothing is missed.
