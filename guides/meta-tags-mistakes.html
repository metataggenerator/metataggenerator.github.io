10 Meta Tag Mistakes That Hurt Your SEO

Meta tag mistakes are rarely dramatic. They are quiet: a duplicated title, a missing description, a social image that never loads. Each one costs a few clicks or a little clarity, and together they can hold a whole site back. The good news is that every mistake on this list is easy to fix once you know to look for it.

Here are the ten most common meta tag mistakes, why each one hurts, and the fix.

Mistakes 1 to 4: duplicates, missing tags, and stuffing

Duplicate titles are the most common error: every page on a site sharing one title, usually the site name. Search engines cannot tell the pages apart, and searchers cannot tell which result to click. Fix it by writing a unique title for every page. Missing descriptions are next: without one, Google writes its own, and the result is often a random fragment.

Keyword stuffing in titles is the third mistake: repeating a phrase three times reads badly and may be rewritten by Google. The fourth is the meta keywords tag stuffed with words, a leftover habit from the 1990s that does nothing today and can look like spam to some engines.

Mistakes 5 to 7: social tags and images

Missing Open Graph tags are the fifth mistake: shared links show a guessed image and a random text fragment instead of a proper card. A relative og:image URL is the sixth: sharing platforms cannot fetch relative paths, so the card shows no image at all. The seventh is an og:image that is too small, which looks blurry when displayed large.

Fix all three by setting the five core Open Graph tags on every page, using absolute image URLs, and sizing images at 1200 by 630 pixels. Test the result in the Sharing Debugger before launch.

Mistakes 8 to 10: technical errors

A stray noindex tag is the eighth mistake and the most damaging: a page that should rank is hidden from search, often after a staging site's tags are copied to the live site. A missing viewport tag is the ninth: the page renders as a tiny desktop layout on phones. A missing charset tag is the tenth: accented characters and apostrophes turn into garbled symbols.

Fix them with a checklist run after every migration or redesign. The technical tags are small, but they are load-bearing: when one is wrong, the whole page suffers.

A quick fix routine

Once a month, crawl your site and look for duplicate titles, missing descriptions, and pages with noindex that should not have it. Fix the worst ten issues, then repeat next month. Within a few months, the list will be short, and the routine will take less than an hour.

Also check your most important pages by hand: the homepage, the top ten landing pages, and any page you are actively promoting. Automated checks catch patterns; manual checks catch the details that matter for the pages that earn your traffic.

Key takeaways

  • Duplicate titles and missing descriptions are the most common errors: fix them first.
  • Use absolute URLs for social images and size them at 1200 by 630.
  • A stray noindex tag can hide a page for weeks: audit after every migration.
  • Check the top ten pages by hand, and crawl the rest monthly.

Frequently asked questions

What is the worst meta tag mistake?

A stray noindex tag on a page that should rank. It removes the page from search entirely, often without anyone noticing for weeks.

Are duplicate titles really a problem?

Yes. Search engines and searchers cannot tell duplicate-titled pages apart, so clicks and ranking value are split or lost.

How do I find meta tag mistakes across a whole site?

Use a website crawler. It reports missing, duplicate, and over-long titles and descriptions for every page at once.
