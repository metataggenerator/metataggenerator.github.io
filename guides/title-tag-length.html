Title Tag Length: How Long Is Too Long?

A title tag can be any length, but Google only shows about 50 to 60 characters of it in search results. Anything longer is cut off with an ellipsis, and the cut can remove the words that made the title worth clicking. Length is therefore one of the few technical details of SEO that directly affects clicks.

This guide explains how Google measures title length, why the limit is in pixels rather than characters, and how to write titles that fit without losing meaning.

How Google measures title length

Google measures titles in pixels, not characters. The limit is about 600 pixels on desktop, which works out to roughly 50 to 60 characters for typical text. Wide characters like capital W and M take more space than narrow ones like i and l, so a title full of capitals may be cut off at 45 characters while a lowercase title fits 65.

Mobile results show slightly less, because the screen is narrower. The practical rule is to aim for 50 to 60 characters and to put the most important words first, so the title still makes sense even if the end is cut off.

What happens when a title is too long

A long title is cut off with an ellipsis, which looks untidy and can hide the key information. Worse, Google may decide to rewrite the title entirely, replacing it with text from the page or from a heading. A rewritten title is usually worse than the one you wrote, because it was not designed as a headline.

Very long titles also dilute the keyword signal. A title with twenty words tells Google less clearly what the page is about than a title with eight focused words. Short, specific titles rank more clearly and read better.

Writing titles that fit

Start with the core phrase: the topic and the angle. Add one modifier, such as a year, a number, or a benefit, and stop. 'Best Budget Laptops 2026: Tested and Ranked' fits; 'The Best Budget Laptops of 2026 for Students, Workers, and Gamers, Tested and Ranked by Experts' does not.

If your brand name must appear, put it at the end after a separator, and be ready to drop it if the title is already long. The brand can be cut off without harm; the topic cannot. Test every title with the Title Tag Checker on this site before publishing.

Length for different page types

Blog posts and guides can use the full 60 characters, because they compete on specificity. Product pages should be shorter and more factual: product name, key feature, and brand. Category pages should name the category and one benefit. Homepages should name the business and what it does, in as few words as possible.

For pages where the title is also the social sharing title, remember that social platforms have their own limits. A title that fits Google usually fits social previews too, but check both if the page will be shared widely.

Key takeaways

  • Aim for 50-60 characters; Google measures in pixels, about 600 on desktop.
  • Put the most important words first, so a cut never removes the key information.
  • Keep the brand name at the end, and drop it if the title is already long.
  • Test every title with a checker before publishing.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a title tag be?

About 50 to 60 characters. Google cuts longer titles off with an ellipsis, and may rewrite titles that are much longer.

Does Google count characters or pixels?

Pixels. Wide characters take more space, so a title with many capitals may be cut off earlier than 60 characters.

What happens if my title is too long?

Google cuts it off with an ellipsis, or rewrites it entirely using text from the page. Either way, you lose control of the headline.
