Open Graph Images: Sizes and Best Practices

When a link is shared, the image is the first thing people notice, often before they read the title. A good Open Graph image earns the click; a bad one, a cropped logo or a blurry photo, makes the link look untrustworthy. The og:image tag is therefore one of the highest-value tags on any page.

This guide covers the sizes, formats, and design rules for Open Graph images, plus the mistakes that make shared links look broken.

The right size and ratio

The recommended size is 1200 by 630 pixels, a ratio of about 1.91 to 1. This is the size Facebook and LinkedIn display best, and it looks sharp on high-resolution screens. The minimum is 200 by 200 pixels, but an image that small will look poor when displayed large.

Keep the file under 8 MB, and ideally under 1 MB, so the sharing platform can fetch it quickly. JPG is best for photographs; PNG is best for graphics with text or sharp edges. Always use an absolute URL, because the platform fetches the image from its own servers and cannot resolve a relative path.

Designing an image that earns clicks

Design the image like a poster, not like a page screenshot. Use one clear photo or illustration, a short headline in large type, and your brand mark small in a corner. Leave generous empty space around the text, because platforms crop images differently on different devices.

Use high contrast between text and background so the headline is readable at small sizes. Test the image at thumbnail size: if you cannot read the headline when the image is 300 pixels wide, shorten it or enlarge the type. Avoid putting essential text near the edges, where cropping can cut it off.

One image per page

Every page deserves its own Open Graph image. A blog post should show an image related to its topic; a product page should show the product; a landing page should show the offer. Using the same logo on every page makes every share look identical, and readers learn to ignore it.

For sites with many pages, a template helps: a consistent layout with a different photo and headline per page. That gives every share a professional look without designing each image from scratch. Many design tools offer social image templates for exactly this purpose.

Common og:image mistakes

The most common mistake is a relative image URL, which sharing platforms cannot fetch. The second is an image that is too small or too large in file size. The third is caching: after fixing an image, the old preview may persist for days until you refresh it with the Sharing Debugger.

Another mistake is using an image with text that is only readable at full size. Social previews are small, especially on phones, so the headline must survive shrinking. Finally, check that the image is not blocked by a login wall or a robots rule, or the platform will show no image at all.

Key takeaways

  • Use 1200 by 630 pixels, JPG for photos and PNG for graphics.
  • Always use an absolute image URL, not a relative path.
  • Design like a poster: one image, one short headline, plenty of empty space.
  • Give every page its own image; never reuse one logo everywhere.

Frequently asked questions

What size should an Open Graph image be?

1200 by 630 pixels is the recommended size. The minimum is 200 by 200, but small images look poor when displayed large.

Why does my shared link show the wrong image?

Usually caching, or a relative URL the platform cannot fetch. Use an absolute URL and refresh the preview in the Sharing Debugger.

Can I use the same og:image on every page?

You can, but you should not. Every page deserves its own image, because identical shares train readers to ignore your links.
