Meta Descriptions: How to Write Ones People Actually Click

The meta description is the short paragraph that appears under your page's title in search results. It is not a ranking factor, but it is the closest thing to a free advertisement on Google: a 160-character pitch that decides whether a searcher clicks your result or the one below it.

This guide explains what makes a description persuasive, how long it should be, and how to write one for every type of page, from blog posts to product pages.

What a meta description does

When your page appears in search results, Google shows the title, the URL, and usually your meta description. If you do not write one, Google writes its own by pulling text from the page, and the result is often a random fragment that does not sell the page. Writing your own description gives you control over the message.

A good description does three things: it confirms the page answers the search, it gives a reason to click now, and it reads like a human wrote it. That is all. It does not need to contain every keyword or list every feature.

How long should a meta description be?

Google usually shows about 150 to 160 characters of a description on desktop, and slightly less on mobile. Aim for 120 to 160 characters: long enough to say something useful, short enough to display fully. If the description is longer, Google cuts it off, and the cut can land in the middle of a sentence.

A structure that works

Start with the answer or the benefit, not with your company name. 'Learn to bake sourdough in a day, with a step-by-step recipe and video' tells the searcher what they get. Then add one specific detail: a number, a time, or a unique feature. End with a gentle call to action when it fits, such as 'Read the guide' or 'See the full list'.

Use active verbs and plain language. Avoid hype words like 'best' or 'amazing' unless you can back them up, because searchers have learned to ignore them. And write each description for its own page: a copied description on fifty pages tells Google nothing about any of them.

Meta descriptions for different page types

Blog posts should promise the answer or the story. Product pages should name the product, its key feature, and a buying reason, such as price, warranty, or free delivery. Category pages should describe the range. Service pages should name the service, the area served, and the outcome.

Homepages are the hardest, because they must cover the whole business in 160 characters. Name what you do, who you do it for, and one reason to choose you. Then move on: the homepage description is rarely the one that earns the most clicks.

Key takeaways

  • Write 120-160 characters that confirm the page answers the search.
  • Start with the benefit or answer, not your company name.
  • Add one specific detail: a number, a time, or a unique feature.
  • Every page needs its own description; never copy one across the site.

Frequently asked questions

Do meta descriptions affect Google rankings?

No, not directly. They affect click-through rate, which is a signal Google watches. A description that earns clicks helps the page hold its position.

What happens if I do not write a meta description?

Google writes one for you by pulling text from the page. The result is often a random fragment that does not persuade anyone to click.

How long should a meta description be?

About 120 to 160 characters. Longer descriptions are cut off, usually in the middle of a sentence.
