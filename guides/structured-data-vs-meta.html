Structured Data vs Meta Tags: What Is the Difference?

Meta tags and structured data both describe a page for machines, and beginners often confuse them. They are related but different tools: meta tags are short labels in the head of a page, while structured data is a detailed description of the page's content, written in a format search engines can read precisely.

This guide explains what each one does, when to use which, and how they work together on a well-built page.

What meta tags do

Meta tags describe the page as a whole: its title, its summary, how it should appear when shared, and whether it should be indexed. They are simple, one-line statements, and they are read by search engines, browsers, and social platforms alike. Every page needs them, and they take minutes to write.

Meta tags cannot describe the details of the content. A meta tag cannot say that a page is a recipe with a cooking time of 30 minutes, or that a product costs 25 dollars and is in stock. That is where structured data comes in.

What structured data does

Structured data, usually written in a format called JSON-LD, describes the content in detail: the type of thing the page is about, its properties, and its values. A recipe's structured data can include ingredients, cooking time, and ratings. A product's can include price, availability, and reviews. An event's can include date, location, and ticket information.

Search engines use structured data to show rich results: star ratings, prices, event dates, and recipe details directly in search results. Those rich results earn more clicks than plain results, which is why structured data is worth adding to important pages.

How they work together

Meta tags and structured data do not compete; they complement each other. The meta tags handle the basics: title, description, social preview, and indexing. Structured data adds the detail that makes rich results possible. A complete page has both.

The two must agree. If the structured data says a product costs 25 dollars and the page shows 30, search engines may ignore the structured data or penalise the page. Keep both updated together whenever the content changes.

When to add structured data

Add structured data to pages where rich results matter: products, recipes, articles, events, jobs, FAQs, and local businesses. Start with your most valuable page types and expand from there. Google's Rich Results Test shows whether your structured data is valid and which rich results it can produce.

You do not need structured data on every page. A simple contact page gains little from it. Focus on the pages that earn traffic and revenue, and keep the markup accurate and up to date.

Key takeaways

  • Meta tags describe the page; structured data describes the content in detail.
  • Structured data enables rich results: ratings, prices, dates, and more.
  • Use both: meta tags for the basics, structured data for the detail.
  • Keep structured data accurate and consistent with the visible page.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between meta tags and structured data?

Meta tags are short labels describing the page as a whole. Structured data is a detailed, machine-readable description of the page's content, such as a product's price or a recipe's cooking time.

Do I need both meta tags and structured data?

Yes, on important pages. Meta tags handle titles, descriptions, and social previews; structured data enables rich results in search.

What format should structured data use?

JSON-LD is the format Google recommends. It sits in a script tag in the head or body and is easy to add and test.
