Meta Tags for Ecommerce: Product Pages That Rank and Sell

A product page is not a blog post, and its meta tags should not read like one. A shopper searching for a product wants facts: what it is, what it costs, whether it is in stock, and why this store. A title and description that answer those questions will beat a clever one every time.

This guide gives you practical formulas for product, category, and homepage meta tags on an ecommerce store, plus the technical tags that matter for shops.

Product page title formula

A good product title tag follows a pattern: product name, key feature or variant, and brand. For example, 'Men's Running Shoes — Lightweight Mesh | RunFast'. Keep it under 60 characters, and put the product name first, because that is what shoppers search for.

Include the variant when it matters: size, colour, or model. A shopper searching for a red kettle wants to land on the red kettle, not the category page. But do not stuff the title with every variant: pick the one that defines the product, and let the page content handle the rest.

Product page description formula

A product description has 120 to 160 characters to do three things: name the product, give one buying reason, and add a nudge. For example, 'Lightweight mesh running shoes with cushioned soles. Free delivery and 30-day returns. Shop now.' The buying reason might be price, warranty, free delivery, or stock availability.

Use real numbers where you can: a price, a discount, or a delivery time. Numbers stand out in a list of text results and give shoppers a concrete reason to click. Update descriptions when prices or offers change, or the result will promise something the page no longer offers.

Category and homepage tags

Category pages should name the category, the range, and one benefit: 'Women's Running Shoes: 200+ Styles with Free Delivery | RunFast'. The category name comes first, because that is the search term. Avoid listing every brand in the title; the page content and filters handle that.

The homepage should name the store, what it sells, and one reason to choose it: 'RunFast: Running Shoes and Gear with Free Delivery'. The homepage rarely ranks for product searches, so its job is branding and trust, not keyword targeting.

Technical tags for stores

Every product page needs a canonical tag pointing at its clean address, without tracking parameters or sort options. Faceted navigation can create hundreds of duplicate addresses for one category, and canonical tags keep the ranking value on the main page. Use noindex on filter combinations that add no value, such as sorting by price alone.

Add structured data for products: price, availability, and reviews. It enables rich results with prices and star ratings in search, which earn more clicks than plain results. And set Open Graph tags with a product image, so shares on social media show the product, not the logo.

Key takeaways

  • Product titles: name, key feature, brand — under 60 characters.
  • Product descriptions: name the product, give one buying reason, add a nudge.
  • Use real numbers: prices, discounts, and delivery times stand out.
  • Canonical tags and product structured data matter more for stores than for blogs.

Frequently asked questions

What should a product page title tag include?

The product name first, then a key feature or variant, then the brand. Keep it under 60 characters.

Should product descriptions include the price?

Yes, when the price is stable. A price in the description gives shoppers a concrete reason to click.

How do I handle duplicate product URLs from filters?

Use canonical tags pointing at the clean category or product address, and noindex filter combinations that add no value.
