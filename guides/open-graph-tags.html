Open Graph Tags: Control How Your Pages Look When Shared

When someone shares your page on Facebook, LinkedIn, or WhatsApp, the platform builds a preview card: a title, an image, and a short description. The Open Graph tags on your page decide what that card shows. Without them, the platform guesses, and the guess is often a cropped logo, a random image, or a fragment of navigation text.

This guide explains the five core Open Graph tags, how to write each one, the image rules that matter, and how to test your cards before anyone shares them.

The five core Open Graph tags

The essential tags are og:title, og:description, og:image, og:url, and og:type. The title is the headline of the card, usually the same as the page title but sometimes shorter and punchier. The description is a one-sentence summary, like a meta description but written for social readers.

The image is the most important tag, because it dominates the card visually. The URL should be the canonical address of the page, so shares and likes count toward one address. The type is usually 'website' for normal pages and 'article' for blog posts, which also allows author and publish date tags.

Writing titles and descriptions for sharing

A social title can be shorter and more conversational than a search title, because the reader is scrolling a feed, not searching. Keep it under 60 characters so it displays fully, and make it work without the context of a search query. A title that says 'The Sourdough Guide' means little alone; 'Bake Your First Sourdough in a Day' works on its own.

The description should be one sentence that gives a reason to click, written for a casual reader. Avoid repeating the title, and avoid jargon. If the page is an article, the description can mention what the reader will learn; if it is a product, mention the key benefit.

The og:image rules that matter

The recommended image size is 1200 by 630 pixels, a ratio of about 1.91 to 1. Smaller images still work, but they may appear small or blurry on high-resolution screens. Use JPG or PNG, keep the file under a few megabytes, and use an absolute URL, not a relative path, because the sharing platform fetches the image from its own servers.

Design the image like a poster: a clear photo or illustration, a short headline, and plenty of empty space. Avoid putting essential text near the edges, because platforms crop images differently. And use a different image per page: a shared logo on every page makes every share look identical.

Testing your Open Graph tags

Facebook's Sharing Debugger and LinkedIn's Post Inspector both show exactly how your page will appear when shared, and they let you refresh the cached preview after you fix a tag. Test every important page before launch, and re-test after changing an image, because platforms cache previews for days.

If a preview shows the wrong image, the usual cause is caching: the platform fetched the page before the tag was added. Use the debugger to clear the cache. If it shows no image at all, check that the image URL is absolute, publicly accessible, and not blocked by a login or a robots rule.

Key takeaways

  • Set all five core tags: title, description, image, URL, and type.
  • Use a 1200 by 630 pixel image with an absolute URL.
  • Write social titles that work on their own, without a search query for context.
  • Test every important page in the Sharing Debugger before launch.

Frequently asked questions

What are Open Graph tags?

Tags in a page's head that tell Facebook, LinkedIn, WhatsApp, and other platforms what title, image, and description to show when the page is shared.

What size should an og:image be?

1200 by 630 pixels is the recommended size. Use an absolute URL and keep the file reasonably small.

Why does my shared link show the wrong image?

Usually caching: the platform fetched the page before the tag was added. Use Facebook's Sharing Debugger to refresh the cached preview.
